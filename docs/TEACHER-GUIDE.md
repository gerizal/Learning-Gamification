# Teacher / Game Master Guide

**Classroom Quiz Game (Kahoot-style)**

This guide is for teachers who run the quiz in class. You need no training from our team: read the quick start, play one practice game with a colleague, and you are ready.

> Another team is still finishing the teacher's screens. Button names in this guide are generic (**Create game**, **Start**, **Next**, **End**). Where an exact label is quoted, it is marked *(label may differ slightly)*.

- Part A: English (A1 to A8)
- Part B: Bahasa Melayu (B1 to B8)

# Part A: English

## A1. Quick start

### What you need
| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| 1 teacher laptop | Chrome or Edge. |
| 1 projector or big TV | Everyone reads the question, the PIN and the leaderboard from here. |
| Student devices | Phones, tablets or lab PCs, one per student or one per pair. They only need a browser: no app, no account. **No devices?** See A3. |
| Network | Student devices must be able to open the game address that is shown on the projector (usually the same Wi-Fi or school network). |
| Speakers (optional) | For sound effects. |

### Before class (5 minutes)
1. Connect the projector and set the display to **duplicate/mirror**.
2. On the laptop, open the game home page `/` (for example `http://<server>/`).
3. Pick the language (**EN / BM**) at the top. This only changes the buttons; questions stay in English.
4. Test that one student device can open the same address.

### Teacher: run a game
1. On `/`, click **I'm a Teacher**.
2. **Pick a pack**, for example *AI Basics / Asas AI*. Open **Why teach this?** to read a short explanation you can share with the class.
3. Every question is multiple choice (true/false is multiple choice with two options). Choose the number of questions (5 / 10 / 15). Optionally type your name, school and program (e.g. *Lenovo Malaysia*, *Apptitude*).
4. Click **Create game**. A big **PIN** appears on the projector.
5. Wait while nicknames appear on the screen. You can remove or rename an unsuitable nickname.
6. When everyone is in, click **Start**.
7. For each question, the game goes through the same four screens:
   - **Question**: the question and a countdown. The screen shows "N / M answered". You can end the timer early if everyone has answered *(label may differ slightly)*.
   - **Reveal**: the correct answer and how many students got it right. Explain it here, in 1–2 sentences.
   - **Leaderboard**: the top 5.
   - Click **Next** to go on.
8. After the last question comes the **podium** (top 3) and "N students joined · M answered at least once".
9. Click **Download CSV** *(label may differ slightly)*. Keep the file for your program report (see A5).

You can click **End** at any time to finish early and go to the podium.

### Students: join a game
1. Open the same address `/` on their device.
2. Tap **I'm a Student**.
3. Type the **PIN** from the projector, then a **nickname**.
4. "You're in! Look at the screen" appears. They wait for you to press Start.
5. For each question, they tap one of the coloured answer tiles, then look up at the projector for the result.

If a student's page reloads, they are put back into the game automatically. Students can also join after the game has started; they begin at 0 points.

### How points work (for when students ask)
- A correct answer earns points. A **faster** correct answer earns more (up to double the slowest correct answer).
- A wrong answer or no answer earns 0 points.
- Several correct answers in a row give a **streak** bonus (up to +50%).
- Each student can answer each question only once.

## A2. Sample 30-minute lesson (with script)

Example: *AI Basics* pack, 10 multiple-choice questions, one device per student or pair. The **Say** lines are suggestions. Use Malay or mixed language where it helps.

| Time | Stage | What you do | What you say |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–3 min** | Hook | Ask the question aloud before opening the game. | "Who used Google Maps, a translation app or YouTube this week? Hands up! Those apps use **AI**, artificial intelligence. Today's quiz is about AI, **in English**." |
| **3–8 min** | Join | **I'm a Teacher** → pick the pack → **Create game**. Read the PIN aloud. Walk around and help with joining. | "Open the game page, tap **I'm a Student**, type this PIN, then your first name. Use your real name so I can count you." |
| **8–9 min** | Rules | Show the list of names. Click **Start** when everyone is in. | "Read the question, then tap your answer. Faster is more points, but correct matters most! Look at the big screen after each question." |
| **9–24 min** | Quiz (10 questions) | For each question: Question → Reveal → Leaderboard → **Next**. That is about 1.5 minutes per question. On **Reveal**, spend 20–30 seconds explaining. | On Reveal: "Why is the answer 'Check it another way'? Because AI can make mistakes!" On Leaderboard: "Big clap for the top 5! Everyone else, the next question can change everything!" |
| **24–26 min** | Podium | Show the podium and the participation line. | "Well done, everyone! 29 of you joined and 29 answered. Let's clap for the winners, **and** for everyone." |
| **26–29 min** | Reflect | Ask 2–3 questions. Answers can be in English or Malay. | "Can AI make mistakes? … Yes! So who makes the final decision? … People! Where did you use AI this week?" |
| **29–30 min** | Save | Click **Download CSV**. | (Optional) "Next week we play *Using AI Safely & Smartly*." |

**Short version (15 min):** 5 questions, skip the reflection or make it one question.
**English focus:** use the *Everyday English* pack as a warm-up (5 questions), then an AI pack (5 questions).
**Teacher training:** play the *AI for Teachers* pack with colleagues, with the trainer as host.

## A3. No student devices? Play on one screen (teams)

Use this when students have no phones or the lab is not available. Everything happens on the teacher's laptop and projector, and students play in **teams**.

**Open it:** on the teacher page, click the small link *"No student devices? Play on one screen (teams)"*, or go straight to `/classroom`.

**3-step setup:**
| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1. Class** | Pick your class, or create one (class name, school, teacher, **program**) and paste the student names, one per line. Names are saved for next time. |
| **2. Content** | Pick a pack. |
| **3. Attendance & teams** | Tap absent students. Choose 2–6 teams and the number of rounds. The screen tells you whether everyone present gets a turn. Then create the teams and press **Start**. |

**During the game:** each turn belongs to one team, and the screen suggests a captain (the team member with the fewest turns so far). Tap another name to change it.
- **Multiple-choice question:** the team agrees on an answer (give them about 10 seconds to discuss). The teacher then clicks the option the team chose, or presses **1–4** on the keyboard *(the exact keys may differ slightly; press **?** to check)*.

**Keyboard shortcuts:**
| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1–4** | Choose answer option 1–4 (multiple choice) |
| **C** | Next team captain |
| **P** | Pause or resume the timer |
| **→** | Next question |
| **?** | Show all shortcuts |

Also: **S** skip, **T** tips, **M** mute, **F** fullscreen, **Esc** close.

In this mode, participation is counted per student. **Present** means marked present in Step 3. **Spoke** means the student had at least one attempt. The results screen has **Download CSV**, and **📊 Reports** shows unique students per program (see A5).

## A4. Shy students and fair play

- **Nicknames:** ask for **real first names** (e.g. "Aisyah R"). It is friendlier, it keeps nicknames appropriate, and it makes the CSV useful for counting participation. Remove any unsuitable name in the lobby.
- **Share a device:** pairs or groups of three can share one device and one nickname. Talking about the answer is good learning.
- **Talk about the answer, not the student:** on the Reveal screen, explain the answer and never point out who got it wrong. The leaderboard only shows the top 5, so nobody sees themselves at the bottom.
- **A slow reader is still a winner:** a correct answer always earns points, even when it is slow. Say so: *"Correct is more important than fast."*
- **One-screen mode:** press **S** (Skip) if a student freezes. The screen shows a friendly message, the team gets 0 points for that turn, and nobody is blamed. Say: *"That's okay! You can try next time."*

## A5. How participation is counted, and the CSV

The programs (Lenovo Malaysia, Apptitude) report **how many students took part**.

**Live quiz (students on devices):**
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Joined** | A nickname that joined the game (and was not removed). |
| **Answered at least once** | The nickname submitted at least one answer. This is the main participation number. |

- At the end, click **Download CSV** *(label may differ slightly)*. The file has one row per nickname: nickname, questions answered, correct answers, average accuracy, score and rank, plus the game details (title, teacher, school, program, date).
- Fill in **teacher, school and program** when you create the game, and ask for **real first names**. Otherwise the CSV cannot be matched to your class.
- Pairs sharing a device appear as **one** nickname. If your program counts individual students, note how many students were in each pair, or give each student their own device.
- Send the CSV to your program coordinator if they ask.

**One-screen team mode (`/classroom`):** *Present* means marked present in attendance. *Spoke* means the student had at least one attempt; a skipped turn does not count. *Participation rate* = spoke ÷ present. Download the CSV from the results screen. The **📊 Reports** page shows unique students present and students who spoke, per program and date range.

## A6. Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Students cannot open the game page** | Check they are on the same network as the laptop/server and typed the address exactly as it appears on the projector. Try a lab PC first to rule out phone data or Wi-Fi limits. |
| **"Wrong PIN" / game not found** | Check the digits on the projector. A PIN stops working once a game has **ended**; create a new game. |
| **"Name taken"** | Two students chose the same nickname. Add a letter, e.g. "Aisyah R". |
| **A student's page froze or reloaded** | Reload the page. The student is put back into the game with the same score. |
| **"N / M answered" is stuck** | Some students have not answered. Wait for the countdown, or end the timer early. Answers after the time limit are not accepted. |
| **Teacher's browser closed by accident** | Open the teacher page again on the same laptop and browser. If the game doesn't come back, click **End** if you can, then create a new game. Students join the new PIN. |
| **"Server not reachable"** | The game server is not running. Ask the person who set it up to start it, wait 30 seconds, then reload. |

## A7. Why teach AI? (talking points for principals and teachers)

*"AI is not in my curriculum targets. Why should I spend class time on it?"*

1. **It is still English lesson time.** Every question is read in English. Students practise reading, vocabulary and quick comprehension. AI is the *topic*; English is the skill.
2. **Students already use AI every day.** Map apps, translation, video and music recommendations, voice assistants, and face unlock all use AI. Few students have been told how these tools work or when they go wrong.
3. **It builds critical thinking.** The core message is: *AI can make mistakes, so people check the answer and make the final decision.* This helps with homework, news and social media.
4. **It keeps students safe.** The *Using AI Safely & Smartly* pack covers scams, deepfakes, passwords, privacy, checking facts before sharing, and kindness online. Parents and schools already worry about these topics.
5. **It prepares students for future jobs.** Technology is changing many kinds of work. Students who understand the basics and can communicate in English will be better prepared.
6. **It fits national directions.** Digital skills and digital literacy are part of the digital education efforts in both Malaysia and Indonesia. A short, fun AI quiz supports that without extra workload.
7. **It helps teachers too.** The *AI for Teachers* pack covers using AI for lesson planning, checking AI output, and simple classroom rules, so teachers feel confident before they teach it.
8. **Participation is measurable.** The game shows how many students joined and answered, and you can download the numbers as a CSV.
9. **It is low cost.** It runs in a browser: no app, no student accounts. It works on lab PCs or phones, or with one screen and teams when there are no devices.

**One sentence for the principal:** *"It is a 30-minute English quiz in which students also learn how the AI in their phones works, where it goes wrong, and how to stay safe, and we can show exactly how many students took part."*

## A8. Train-the-trainer checklist

For a teacher who is showing the game to another teacher. The new teacher does every step **themselves**. A good way to practise is for the trainer and 2–3 colleagues to play as "students" on their phones while the new teacher hosts the *AI for Teachers* pack.

**Before the session (the trainer)**
- [ ] The game home page `/` opens on the laptop and on at least one phone or PC on the school network.
- [ ] The projector and speakers are ready.

**The new teacher can…**
- [ ] Connect the projector and mirror the screen.
- [ ] Open `/`, switch the language, click **I'm a Teacher**.
- [ ] Pick a pack and explain "Why teach this?" in one sentence.
- [ ] Choose question types and a number of questions, and fill in teacher, school and program.
- [ ] **Create game** and read the PIN aloud clearly.
- [ ] Guide students to join: `/` → **I'm a Student** → PIN → real first name.
- [ ] Remove or rename an unsuitable nickname in the lobby.
- [ ] **Start** and run each question: Question → Reveal (explain the answer) → Leaderboard → **Next**.
- [ ] End the timer early when everyone has answered.
- [ ] Use **End** to finish early.
- [ ] Read the participation line on the podium and **download the CSV**.
- [ ] Solve "wrong PIN", "name taken" and "student page froze".
- [ ] Run a short game in one-screen team mode (`/classroom`): create a class, take attendance, make teams, and choose an answer for a team (click it, or use keys 1–4).

**Sign-off**
- [ ] The new teacher has hosted one full practice game (at least 5 questions) **alone**, without help.
- [ ] They know who to contact if the server is not running.
- [ ] They have a copy of this guide.

# Part B: Bahasa Melayu

> Pasukan lain masih menyiapkan skrin guru. Nama butang dalam panduan ini adalah umum (**Create game**, **Start**, **Next**, **End**). Label yang dipetik ditanda *(label mungkin sedikit berbeza)*.

## B1. Mula pantas

### Apa yang anda perlukan
| Barang | Catatan |
|---|---|
| 1 komputer riba guru | Chrome atau Edge. |
| 1 projektor atau TV besar | Semua orang membaca soalan, PIN dan papan markah di sini. |
| Peranti murid | Telefon, tablet atau PC makmal, satu untuk setiap murid atau setiap pasangan. Hanya perlukan pelayar: tiada aplikasi, tiada akaun. **Tiada peranti?** Lihat B3. |
| Rangkaian | Peranti murid mesti boleh membuka alamat permainan yang dipaparkan pada projektor (biasanya Wi-Fi atau rangkaian sekolah yang sama). |
| Pembesar suara (pilihan) | Untuk kesan bunyi. |

### Sebelum kelas (5 minit)
1. Sambungkan projektor dan tetapkan paparan kepada **duplicate/mirror**.
2. Pada komputer riba, buka halaman utama `/`.
3. Pilih bahasa (**EN / BM**). Ini hanya menukar butang; soalan kekal dalam bahasa Inggeris.
4. Uji bahawa satu peranti murid boleh membuka alamat yang sama.

### Guru: jalankan permainan
1. Di `/`, klik **I'm a Teacher** (Saya Guru).
2. **Pilih pek**, contohnya *AI Basics / Asas AI*. Buka **Why teach this?** *(label mungkin sedikit berbeza)* untuk membaca penerangan ringkas.
3. Semua soalan ialah pilihan jawapan (benar/salah ialah pilihan jawapan dengan dua pilihan). Pilih bilangan soalan (5 / 10 / 15). Isi nama guru, sekolah dan program jika mahu.
4. Klik **Create game**. **PIN** besar dipaparkan pada projektor.
5. Nama murid akan muncul di skrin. Anda boleh membuang atau menukar nama yang tidak sesuai.
6. Apabila semua sudah masuk, klik **Start**.
7. Setiap soalan melalui empat skrin yang sama:
   - **Soalan**: soalan dan kiraan detik, serta "N / M sudah menjawab". Anda boleh menamatkan masa lebih awal jika semua sudah menjawab.
   - **Jawapan**: jawapan betul dan berapa ramai yang betul. Terangkan dalam 1–2 ayat.
   - **Papan markah**: 5 teratas.
   - Klik **Next** untuk teruskan.
8. Selepas soalan terakhir: **podium** (3 teratas) dan "N murid menyertai · M menjawab sekurang-kurangnya sekali".
9. Klik **Download CSV** *(label mungkin sedikit berbeza)* dan simpan fail untuk laporan program (lihat B5).

Klik **End** pada bila-bila masa untuk tamat awal.

### Murid: masuk permainan
1. Buka alamat `/` yang sama pada peranti.
2. Ketik **I'm a Student** (Saya Murid).
3. Taip **PIN** dari projektor, kemudian **nama panggilan**.
4. Tunggu guru menekan Start.
5. Untuk setiap soalan, ketik satu jubin jawapan berwarna, kemudian lihat keputusan di projektor.

Jika halaman murid dimuat semula, murid akan kembali ke permainan secara automatik. Murid juga boleh masuk selepas permainan bermula, dengan 0 markah.

### Cara markah dikira
- Jawapan betul mendapat markah, dan jawapan betul yang **lebih cepat** mendapat lebih banyak markah.
- Jawapan salah atau tiada jawapan mendapat 0.
- Beberapa jawapan betul berturut-turut memberi bonus **streak** (sehingga +50%).
- Setiap murid hanya boleh menjawab sekali bagi setiap soalan.

## B3. Tiada peranti murid? Main pada satu skrin (pasukan)

Gunakan mod ini apabila murid tiada telefon atau makmal tidak tersedia. Semuanya berlaku pada komputer riba guru dan projektor, dan murid bermain dalam **pasukan**.

**Buka:** klik pautan kecil *"No student devices? Play on one screen (teams)"* pada halaman guru, atau pergi terus ke `/classroom`.

**Persediaan 3 langkah:** (1) **Kelas**: pilih atau cipta kelas (nama kelas, sekolah, guru, **program**), dan tampal nama murid. (2) **Kandungan**: pilih pek. (3) **Kehadiran & pasukan**: ketik murid yang tidak hadir, pilih 2–6 pasukan dan bilangan pusingan, kemudian mula.

**Semasa permainan:** setiap giliran milik satu pasukan.
- **Soalan pilihan jawapan:** pasukan berbincang (kira-kira 10 saat) dan bersetuju. Guru kemudian klik pilihan yang dipilih pasukan, atau tekan **1–4** pada papan kekunci *(kekunci mungkin sedikit berbeza; tekan **?** untuk semak)*.

**Pintasan:** **1–4** pilih jawapan · **C** kapten seterusnya · **P** jeda pemasa · **→** soalan seterusnya · **?** semua pintasan. Juga: **S** langkau, **T** tip, **M** senyap, **F** skrin penuh.

Penyertaan dalam mod ini: *Hadir* bermaksud ditanda hadir; *Bercakap* bermaksud murid ada sekurang-kurangnya satu cubaan. Muat turun CSV di skrin keputusan; **📊 Laporan** menunjukkan murid unik mengikut program.

## B4. Murid pemalu dan permainan adil

- **Nama panggilan:** minta murid guna **nama pertama sebenar** (cth. "Aisyah R"). Buang nama yang tidak sesuai di lobi.
- **Kongsi peranti:** berpasangan atau bertiga dengan satu nama. Berbincang tentang jawapan ialah pembelajaran yang baik.
- **Bincang jawapan, bukan murid:** jangan sebut siapa yang salah. Papan markah hanya menunjukkan 5 teratas.
- **Betul lebih penting daripada cepat:** jawapan betul sentiasa mendapat markah.
- **Mod satu skrin:** tekan **S** (Langkau) jika murid tidak bersedia. Sebut: *"Tak apa! Cuba lagi lain kali."*

## B5. Cara penyertaan dikira, dan fail CSV

**Kuiz langsung:**
| Istilah | Maksud |
|---|---|
| **Menyertai** | Nama panggilan yang masuk ke permainan (dan tidak dibuang). |
| **Menjawab sekurang-kurangnya sekali** | Nama panggilan itu menghantar sekurang-kurangnya satu jawapan. Ini angka penyertaan utama. |

- Klik **Download CSV** di akhir permainan. Satu baris untuk setiap nama: nama panggilan, bilangan soalan dijawab, jawapan betul, purata ketepatan, markah dan kedudukan, serta maklumat permainan (tajuk, guru, sekolah, program, tarikh).
- Isi **guru, sekolah dan program** semasa mencipta permainan, dan minta **nama pertama sebenar**.
- Pasangan yang berkongsi peranti dikira sebagai **satu** nama. Catat bilangan murid dalam setiap pasangan jika program anda mengira murid secara individu.

**Mod satu skrin (`/classroom`):** *Hadir* = ditanda hadir; *Bercakap* = sekurang-kurangnya satu cubaan (giliran yang dilangkau tidak dikira); *Kadar* = bercakap ÷ hadir. Halaman **📊 Laporan** menunjukkan murid unik mengikut program dan tarikh.

## B7. Mengapa ajar AI? (perkara utama untuk pengetua dan guru)

*"AI bukan sasaran kurikulum saya. Mengapa saya perlu menggunakan masa kelas untuknya?"*

1. **Ia tetap masa pelajaran bahasa Inggeris.** Setiap soalan dibaca dalam bahasa Inggeris: membaca, kosa kata dan pemahaman pantas. AI ialah *topik*; bahasa Inggeris ialah kemahirannya.
2. **Murid sudah menggunakan AI setiap hari:** aplikasi peta, terjemahan, cadangan video dan muzik, pembantu suara, buka kunci dengan wajah.
3. **Ia membina pemikiran kritis.** *AI boleh tersilap, jadi manusia menyemak jawapan dan membuat keputusan akhir.*
4. **Ia menjaga keselamatan murid:** penipuan (scam), deepfake, kata laluan, privasi, menyemak fakta sebelum berkongsi, dan berbudi bahasa dalam talian.
5. **Ia menyediakan murid untuk pekerjaan masa depan.** Teknologi sedang mengubah banyak jenis pekerjaan.
6. **Ia selari dengan hala tuju negara.** Kemahiran dan literasi digital ialah sebahagian daripada usaha pendidikan digital di Malaysia dan Indonesia.
7. **Ia membantu guru juga.** Pek *AI for Teachers* merangkumi AI untuk merancang pelajaran, menyemak hasil AI dan peraturan bilik darjah yang mudah.
8. **Penyertaan boleh diukur:** berapa ramai yang menyertai dan menjawab, dan angkanya boleh dimuat turun sebagai CSV.
9. **Kos rendah:** berjalan dalam pelayar, tanpa aplikasi dan tanpa akaun murid. Boleh dimainkan pada PC makmal, telefon, atau satu skrin dengan pasukan.

**Satu ayat untuk pengetua:** *"Ini kuiz bahasa Inggeris selama 30 minit. Murid juga belajar bagaimana AI dalam telefon mereka berfungsi, bila ia tersilap dan cara kekal selamat, dan kita boleh tunjukkan dengan tepat berapa ramai murid yang mengambil bahagian."*

## B8. Senarai semak latih-jurulatih (train-the-trainer)

Guru baharu melakukan setiap langkah **sendiri**. Cara terbaik untuk berlatih: jurulatih dan 2–3 rakan sekerja bermain sebagai "murid" dengan telefon, manakala guru baharu menjadi hos untuk pek *AI for Teachers*.

**Guru baharu boleh…**
- [ ] Menyambung projektor dan memaparkan skrin yang sama (mirror).
- [ ] Membuka `/`, menukar bahasa, dan klik **I'm a Teacher**.
- [ ] Memilih pek dan menerangkan "Why teach this?" dalam satu ayat.
- [ ] Memilih jenis dan bilangan soalan, serta mengisi guru, sekolah dan program.
- [ ] **Create game** dan membacakan PIN dengan jelas.
- [ ] Membimbing murid masuk: `/` → **I'm a Student** → PIN → nama pertama sebenar.
- [ ] Membuang atau menukar nama yang tidak sesuai di lobi.
- [ ] **Start** dan menjalankan setiap soalan: Soalan → Jawapan (terangkan) → Papan markah → **Next**.
- [ ] Menamatkan masa lebih awal apabila semua sudah menjawab, dan menggunakan **End** untuk tamat awal.
- [ ] Membaca angka penyertaan pada podium dan **memuat turun CSV**.
- [ ] Menyelesaikan masalah "PIN salah", "nama sudah diambil" dan "halaman murid tersekat".
- [ ] Menjalankan permainan pendek dalam mod satu skrin (`/classroom`): cipta kelas, kehadiran, pasukan, dan memilih jawapan pasukan (klik, atau kekunci 1–4).

**Pengesahan**
- [ ] Guru baharu telah menjadi hos untuk satu permainan latihan penuh (sekurang-kurangnya 5 soalan) **sendiri**.
- [ ] Guru tahu siapa yang perlu dihubungi jika pelayan tidak berjalan.
- [ ] Guru mempunyai salinan panduan ini.
